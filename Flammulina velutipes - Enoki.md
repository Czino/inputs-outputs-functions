Enoki (_Flammulina velutipes_) is a cold-tolerant fungus cultivated on hardwood sawdust; grown under light it forms short-stemmed, normally-capped mushrooms, while darkness and cold produce the long, pale stems sold commercially.[^1]

## Inputs
- Substrate: spruce sawdust and rice bran, 3:1 by volume, moistened to 65% water content, packed into culture bottles around a central air hole, and autoclaved (121°C, 20 minutes).[^1]
- Spawn: sawdust-based spawn colonizes and fruits faster and more heavily than agar-disc spawn.[^1]
- Spawn run: 25°C in the dark until mycelium fully covers the substrate, about 10-12 days.[^1]
- Fruiting trigger: after spawn run, scraping off the old surface mycelium ("kinkaki") and soaking the surface with water promotes more numerous, more evenly-timed fruiting.[^1]
- Fruiting: 5-10°C; about 30-45 days from inoculation to harvest. Light during fruiting produces short-stemmed, normally-capped mushrooms; constant darkness produces the long, thin-stemmed, pale form.[^1]
