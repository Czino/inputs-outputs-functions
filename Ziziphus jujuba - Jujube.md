Jujube trees produce small, date-like fruits that can be eaten fresh or dried. They are grown for their edible fruits and ornamental value. It reaches 5-11 m tall and 3-9 m wide at maturity.[^3] Jujube is sometimes listed as a nitrogen fixer, but this appears to be a mix-up with other members of the buckthorn family (Rhamnaceae): of that family's roughly 50 genera, only *Ceanothus*, *Colletia*, *Discaria*, *Kentrothamnus*, *Retanilla*, and *Trevoa* are confirmed to form nitrogen-fixing symbioses with *Frankia* bacteria — *Ziziphus* is not among them.[^4]
