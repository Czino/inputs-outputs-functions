Creeping buttercup (_Ranunculus repens_) is a mat-forming perennial herb native to [[Europe|Europe]] and temperate [[Asia|Asia]], spreading rapidly by rooting stolons through wet meadows, pastures and woods.[^1] All parts are poisonous and the acrid juice can blister skin; the toxins are destroyed by heat or drying.[^1] Its leaves and dried root have been eaten as a last-resort famine food, but this is discouraged even then.[^1]
