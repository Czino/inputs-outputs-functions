Bird of paradise shrub (_Erythrostemon gilliesii_, syn. _Caesalpinia gilliesii_) is a deciduous shrub native to [[South America|Argentina and Uruguay]], grown for its showy red-and-yellow flowers. It reaches 2.5-4 m tall and 1.5-2.5 m wide.[^2] Its green seed pods are reported to severely irritate the digestive tract if eaten.[^1] Despite being a legume, it does not fix nitrogen: a field and pot-inoculation survey of Balochistan legumes found it to be a non-nodulator, consistent with nodulation being uncommon outside the mimosoid/Papilionoideae clades of the family.[^3]
