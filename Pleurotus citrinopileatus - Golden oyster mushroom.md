Golden oyster mushroom (_Pleurotus citrinopileatus_) is a bright-yellow-capped relative of [[Pleurotus ostreatus - Oyster mushroom|oyster mushroom]], cultivated on wheat straw for its colour, flavour, and bioactive compound content; in parts of North America it has also escaped cultivation and spread aggressively on dead hardwood, displacing native wood-decay fungi.[^1][^2]

## Inputs
- Substrate: wheat straw chaff (1-2 cm particle size), wetted to 70% moisture content, sterilised at 121°C for 1 hour; can be enriched with wheat bran or other agricultural by-products.[^1]
- Spawn run: grain spawn inoculated into the cooled substrate, incubated at 25°C and 80-85% relative humidity until fully colonised.[^1]
- Fruiting: 21°C, 85-90% relative humidity, 500 lux light for 12 hours a day, CO2 held below 1,000 ppm.[^1]

## Outputs
- [[Human food]]: cultivated for its fruiting bodies, prized for their attractive colour.[^1]
- [[Medicinal use]]: fruit bodies contain bioactive triacylglycerols, terpenes, steroids, glucans, polysaccharides, and phenolic compounds with documented antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antihyperglycaemic, and immunostimulatory properties.[^1]

## Type
- [[Edible fungi]]

## Functions
- None known as a beneficial function in this vault's sense — in parts of North America (confirmed around Minnesota and the upper Midwest) it has escaped cultivation and established on dead hardwood in the wild, where it reduces native wood-decay fungal diversity on colonised logs; worth factoring into any decision to grow it outdoors rather than in an enclosed system.[^2]
