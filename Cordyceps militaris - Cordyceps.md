Cordyceps (_Cordyceps militaris_) is a medicinal fungus grown axenically on a sterile rice substrate rather than on its traditional insect host, prized for its content of cordycepin and other bioactive compounds.[^1]

## Inputs
- Substrate: rice (fresh or aged), 40 g dry material per 500 mL culture bottle at 60% water content, sterilized at 121°C for 60 minutes.[^1]
- Spawn: 8-10% (w/w) grain spawn inoculated onto the cooled substrate surface.[^1]
- Spawn run: dark, 18-22°C, until white mycelium fully covers the substrate.[^1]
- Fruiting initiation: 25±1°C, diffuse light at 800-1,000 lux on a 12-hour light/dark cycle, 70-80% relative humidity.[^1]
- Fruiting: once primordia form, holes are punched in the bottle covering and humidity raised to 85-95% at 18-22°C with diffuse light, until fruiting bodies swell and harvest-ready asci appear.[^1]

## Outputs
- [[Human food]]: fruiting bodies tested safe for food use, rich in protein.[^1]
- [[Medicinal use]]: fruiting bodies contain cordycepin, polysaccharides, polyphenols, glycoproteins, superoxide dismutase, and ergosterol.[^1]
