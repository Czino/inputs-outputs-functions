Japanese privet (_Ligustrum japonicum_) is an evergreen shrub native to woods and thickets of lowland and hill [[Asia|East Asia]] (China, Japan, Korea and Taiwan).[^1] Its leaves and shoots are edible and its roasted seed is used as a [[Coffee substitute]], though cooked young shoots are only a famine food and are probably toxic, since they contain a glycoside.[^1] It grows up to 5 m tall and 6 m wide.[^1]
