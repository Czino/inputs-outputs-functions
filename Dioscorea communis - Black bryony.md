Black bryony (_Dioscorea communis_) is a dioecious perennial climber native to [[Europe]], North Africa and western [[Asia]], growing in hedgerows, scrub and woodland edges. The whole plant is poisonous, mainly from calcium oxalate crystals concentrated in the fruit; the young shoots are edible only after cooking, and the root is used medicinally as an external poultice only, under expert guidance.[^1]
