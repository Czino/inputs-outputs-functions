Wood ear (_Auricularia auricula-judae_) is a gelatinous, ear-shaped fungus grown on hardwood logs or a composted sawdust-and-bran substrate, eaten in Chinese cuisine and traditionally credited with medicinal effects.[^1][^2]

## Inputs
- Substrate: composted sawdust (78%), bran (20%), calcium carbonate (1%), and sucrose (1%); or cottonseed hulls (93%), wheat bran (5%), sucrose (1%), and calcium carbonate (1%) used directly without composting; or natural logs of broad-leaf trees, with oaks preferred.[^1]
- Spawn run: about 25°C (±2°C) for 28-30 days, kept below 500 lux — more light during this stage can trigger premature fruiting.[^1]

## Outputs
- [[Human food]][^2]
- [[Medicinal use]][^2]
