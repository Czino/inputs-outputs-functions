Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) are soil fungi that colonize the roots of 80-90% of vascular plant families, trading soil nutrients for plant sugars.[^1]

## Inputs
- Root contact with a host plant; spores have only a short window (days to weeks) to colonize a root before they die.[^1]
- Avoid heavy tillage, synthetic fertilizer, and fungicide use, all of which suppress colonization; reduced/conservation tillage and seed drills favor it.[^1]

## Outputs
- Improved plant uptake of phosphorus, nitrogen, sulfur, potassium, copper, and iron, in exchange for about 20% of the host plant's sugars.[^1]

## Type
- [[Mycorrhizal fungi]]

## Functions
- [[Nutrient transport]][^1]
- [[Soil building]][^1]
- [[Drought-tolerance]] — AMF improve water-use efficiency and can increase leaf area under drought stress.[^1]
